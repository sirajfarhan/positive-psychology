# The Dimensions and How to Score Them

Four rows: three carry the validated instrument, and the fourth is a
different measure, marked as one everywhere it appears.

## What gets scored

The unit is a **causal statement**, not a session and not a mood. A person
describes something that happened and says, explicitly or otherwise, why. That
"why" is the scoreable object. One conversation may yield several; a long
account with no causal claim in it yields none.

Every statement carries a **valence**: the event it explains either went
well (`good`) or badly (`bad`). Valence is a property of the event, not of the
explanation.

## The scale

Each dimension scores **1 to 7 on the raw CAVE convention**:

```
7  permanent    general     personal
4  neutral      neutral     neutral
1  temporary    specific    situational
```

Store the raw score. Never store the oriented one. Orientation depends on
valence and is computed at read time.

## The three

### Permanence: is the cause still running?

The tell is **trait versus event**. A trait has no end date; an event has
already finished.

| Raw | Setback | Win |
|---|---|---|
| 7 | "I'm bad with contracts." | "I'm good at this." |
| 5 | "I keep doing this." | "I usually get these right." |
| 4 | "That was handled badly." | "That went well." |
| 2 | "I was slow on that one." | "I moved fast on that one." |
| 1 | "I didn't set terms on that one." | "The timing happened to line up." |

Watch for *always*, *never*, *I'm the kind of person who*.

For a **setback**, low is optimistic. For a **win**, high is optimistic.

### Pervasiveness: how much does the cause cover?

The poles are **general** and **specific**. (The 1978 paper calls the wide pole
*global*; `general` is the word this system shows, and they mean the same
thing.)

Does the cause named reach one area, or everything?

| Raw | Setback | Win |
|---|---|---|
| 7 | "I'm not a serious operator." | "I'm good at all of this." |
| 5 | "I'm not built for the client-facing side." | "I'm good with clients." |
| 4 | "My commercial judgment was off." | "My commercial judgment was good." |
| 2 | "I misread that account." | "I read that account well." |
| 1 | "I structured that one deal badly." | "That one pitch landed." |

The same facts can be stated either way. One version leaks into the marriage
and the sleep; the other stays where it happened.

**Score the cause named, not the leak observed.** If someone explains a lost
account with "I'm not built for client work" and separately says "the whole
business is sliding", the second sentence is evidence that the cause is
spreading. But it carries no causal claim, so it does not set the score. Score
the first at the width of what it names. Record the second in `note`. The
verbatim causal sentence always decides.

For a **setback**, low is optimistic. For a **win**, high is optimistic.

### Personalization: a cause, or a defect?

This is the one that gets misread, so read the anchors carefully.

**High scorers own more, not less.** The optimistic move is naming your
specific contribution without converting it into a verdict on yourself. It is
not blaming circumstances.

| Raw | Setback |
|---|---|
| 7 | "I'm an idiot." — you *are* the problem |
| 5 | "I always do this." — you, plus permanence |
| 3 | "I set it on a handshake, and they chose not to pay." — your part, plus other causes |
| 1 | "Nothing I could have done." — no agency at all |

But **1 is not the target** here. A statement with no ownership in it scores
low on personalization and is its own failure mode. It buys optimism by
giving up agency, which is exactly the trade Sage's To-Me mode makes. The
healthy region for a setback is roughly 2 to 3: specific ownership, no verdict.

For a **win**, the direction is simpler and the reverse: crediting yourself is
optimistic, crediting luck is not.

| Raw | Win |
|---|---|
| 7 | "I built the thing that made this work." |
| 4 | "It came together." |
| 1 | "Got lucky with the timing." |

### Scoring cheat

```
setback:  oriented = -(raw - 4)      low raw is optimistic
win:      oriented = +(raw - 4)      high raw is optimistic
```

## The fourth: builds

`builds` is **not part of the explanatory-style instrument.** It comes from
Napoleon Hill by way of Peter Sage, who wrote the line above his cell door,
and it maps to a separately validated construct: post-traumatic growth
(Tedeschi & Calhoun's PTGI, 1996), which has its own five factors and its own
psychometrics.

Four things follow, and all of them hold:

- It is scored **only on setbacks.** There is nothing to grow from in a win.
- **It does not flip with valence.** 7 always means more was built.
- It is measured by a different method in the literature, self-report rather
  than content analysis. So scoring it from language is our extension and
  carries less weight than the three above it.
- It is displayed set apart from the three, marked *different measure*. Never
  present it as a peer.

| Raw | Setback |
|---|---|
| 7 | "It cost me the year and bought me the terms discipline I use on everything now." |
| 5 | "I did learn something about how I scope these." |
| 4 | "Something came of it." |
| 2 | "Maybe there's a lesson in there somewhere." |
| 1 | "It's just gone." |

**When the statement is silent about what it built, store nothing.** A missing
score and a score of 1 are different claims: one says they did not address it,
the other says they addressed it and found nothing. Never infer `builds` from a
sentence that carries no causal claim.

**The cost must survive the sentence.** A statement that names a benefit by
erasing the loss is not growth, it is denial, and it scores mid at best. The
7 above works because the year is still in it.

Worth knowing: PTG has a live critique called *illusory growth*. Self-reported
growth frequently exceeds what longitudinal measurement finds. Score what the
sentence says; make no claim that the growth is real.

## Two failure modes to score honestly

**Defensive pessimism.** Some people set deliberately low expectations,
prepare hard, and perform excellently. They score pessimistic across the board
and the strategy works for them. The reading describes their language; it does
not diagnose a problem.

**Bought optimism.** A statement can score well on all three by disowning the
event entirely: nothing lasting, nothing widespread, nothing to do with me.
That is not optimism, it is absence of agency, and personalization near 1 on a
setback is the marker for it. Flag it rather than celebrating the composite.
